# C109: The version is 1.1.0, superseding C103 (6)

*Recorded 2026-11-05, coordinator, the operator's Q1.* · [Decision log](README.md)

## Decision

(1) [C103](C103.md) (6) shipped the separators' predecessor as a patch, 1.0.1. That version was prepared on `main` and never tagged: the batch folds its entry into 1.1.0's, and no 1.0.1 exists. (2) The batch is a minor by the CHANGELOG's own rule: it adds options, operations and a draw mode, and it moves four asset hashes (`assets/frame/bootstrap.js`, `assets/frame/enhance.js`, `assets/frame/reader.css` and `assets/reader.css`), so a host that serves the frame re-pins its page policy. Bridge protocol 1 and seed schema 1 are unchanged (Q11). (3) The release commit sets `package.json` and the lockfile's root to 1.1.0, regenerates `assets/assets.json` with `npm run build:frame`, rewrites the changelog's 1.0.1 entry as 1.1.0's (a test pins its heading to the package version and its four hashes to `assets/assets.json`), and carries the README's status line. (4) Task 9 departed from its brief in three small ways (BX-5 in the plan's table, accepted by the wave's controller and ratified by the coordinator at review 1480): it also moved the tarball name pinned in `test/pack.test.ts`, it added a test that pins the changelog's top entry, and its mutation record holds the four rows that test needed (B9a to B9d). (5) The signed `v1.1.0` tag, and the publish through the release workflow, stay the operator's acts after the pull request merges.

## Why

The operator's Q1 answer, and the CHANGELOG's rule for a moved hash.

## Cost if wrong

A version number; nothing is tagged until the operator tags.
