# Linked spec

**Spec** · 2026-10-20 · Status: Draft

## In short

This example shows how a reader follows a decision such as D2, and how a term such as a fold or a read mark is defined once and named everywhere. Every name in it is a stand-in.

## 1. Decisions

| ID | Decision | Status |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Keep the fold beside its heading | Agreed |
| D2 | Mark a section read once it is seen, as D1 allows | Proposed |
| D3 | Show the read marks in the contents | Later |

D1 and D2 are linked the first time this paragraph names them; a second D1 is plain text. A fold keeps a long section short, and a read mark says what was seen.

D3 links again in a new paragraph, while D9 is not defined anywhere and stays text.

- D2 in a list item links once, even when the item names D2 twice.
- The next item names D3, and so links it.

## 2. Reading

A fold hides the body of a section and keeps its heading. Folds come back with one click, and the read marks follow the reader from section to section.

> A quoted line names D1 and the fold: a quote is a block of its own.

## 3. Words used here

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fold | A section shown as its heading only |
| Read mark (marker) | A mark on a section the reader has seen |
| Box, Label | Small parts of the page, each name matched as spelled |

## 4. Where it ends

A fold and a read mark are linked again in a new section, and so is a marker. A Box and a Label, as spelled, are linked too.
